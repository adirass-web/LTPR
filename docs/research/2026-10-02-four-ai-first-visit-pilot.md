# Four-AI first-visit pilot: integrated editorial profile

Date: 2 October 2026  
Target: [Cloudflare narrative review](https://codex-a3-mobile-review.cyberdrtabansky.pages.dev/lab/single/narrative/) at content commit `5ad54fe`  
Protocol: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-first-visit-review-design.md`

## What was tested and what was not

Four fresh-context AI agents each saw the same **390 × 500 opening crop** before being given a site brief or live URL. The crop shows the identity, portrait, headline and introductory sentence, but intentionally stops before “Selected work.” It was made from an earlier screenshot; the visible opening content was unchanged by the subsequent copy pass. Therefore, the blind response **does not test the whole 390px first viewport** or whether project proof appears before a human's first scroll. It tests only the opening unit. The agents then inspected the current live review page independently through four analytic lenses. They were not real prospective clients, had no lived device/access needs, and their numerical similarity must not be treated as representative evidence or statistical validation.

All ratings use the same 1–5 anchors: 1 absent/contradictory; 2 weak; 3 mixed; 4 clear and supported; 5 unmistakable without overstatement. N/A was allowed. The evidence and disagreements below matter more than the numbers.

## Individual readouts

### R1 — institutional decision-maker lens

**Blind opening.** Read the person as a serious, strategic, polished adviser to governments and institutions. Saw no proof of delivered outcomes in the opening crop; asked what he had delivered and for whom. Expected cases, results and credentials below.

**Live tasks.** Found PROGRESS immediately under “Selected work,” correctly traced “being implemented” to the [2025 university field record](https://rcrl.tau.ac.il/progress_jcp_jan25), and distinguished this from a measured result. Found the national-strategy report in the [university biography](https://en-cyber.tau.ac.il/our_team_rcrl/lior_tabansky), the RAI and Le Figaro media items, the separate Media archive, and email contact. Would inquire about cross-sector resilience and an implementation plan.

**Notable reservations.** Practical evidence is credible but stops short of measured effect. The visitor must infer engagement scope. The long later media sequence can feel detached from client work. No verified false claim; the 11-country headline and implementation language must not be read as completed rollout in every country.

### R2 — international-development / infrastructure lead lens

**Blind opening.** Read the adviser role clearly; described the crop as serious, spare and confident. Saw no concrete outcome yet and expected project evidence after scrolling.

**Live tasks.** Found the 11-country/four-sector PROGRESS work and its source, the national-strategy deliverable, NTV and RAI interviews, Media archive and email. Summarized the method as aligning institutions, data and operating model around a consequential decision so capability survives the assessment. Would ask about adapting PROGRESS to an energy or healthcare programme.

**Notable reservations.** The site does not identify Tabansky's exact role, which recommendations have been implemented, or the implementation stage. AI has a point of view and a press annotation, not a comparably concrete applied example. “World Bank / PROGRESS” should not imply employment or sole ownership. Later warfare, pager and surveillance media may visually outweigh implementation evidence.

### R3 — international editor / communications lens

**Blind opening.** Read the role and ambition; saw no proof of practical success within the crop. Impression: serious, strategic, polished. Asked what specific capabilities were delivered.

**Live tasks.** Correctly separated the PROGRESS field record from measured gains and the national-strategy report from government adoption. Found POLITICO, the full Media archive, and contact. Read the method as decision-first capability-building across organizations and sectors.

**Notable reservations.** The page could make the status of the 2025 implementation claim more prominent. AI delivery is not evidenced like the cyber-resilience work. The explicit Israel paragraph is a little more foregrounded than “subtext.” Forums show international range, but not the role or subject of every appearance. The headline risks a mild consulting aphorism; no stronger cliché was observed.

### R4 — informed non-specialist lens

**Blind opening.** Understood adviser to governments/institutions and an ambition to create usable capabilities. Impression: serious, strategic, restrained. Saw no named proof within the crop and wanted project results and a way to engage.

**Live tasks.** Found the PROGRESS record and its implementation-in-progress qualification, distinguished work from commentary, found RAI and the Media archive, and identified the email route. Could summarize the decision → organization/data/operating capability idea in plain language.

**Notable reservations.** National-strategy work feels generic without role or outcome detail. The AI section is conceptual and paired with warfare commentary rather than an applied case. Some later images add public visibility more than understanding of the work. The exact engagement scope and outcome of the 11-country project remain unclear.

## Synthesis against the site brief

The core positioning survives the pilot: all four agents could explain who this person is, articulate the systems/decision method, find public applied work, and distinguish that work from interviews and commentary. They found the separate Media archive and a contact route. There was no clear factual overclaim in the claims they checked against the university records.

The principal gap is **authority with demonstrated practical success**. The authority half is strong. The practical half shows credible application and a reported implementation process, but not a verifiable change in resilience or a clearly stated individual role at each stage. The current copy is careful; the risk is what a reader may infer from “capabilities that work,” “World Bank / PROGRESS,” and the 11-country scope. Do not invent results to fill the gap.

The second gap is balance: cyber resilience has a substantial public project record, while AI is supported chiefly by a well-formed method and a commentary item. In the long latter part of the narrative, press examples can begin to compete visually with the two work records. **This is not an image-count finding.** Keep all useful archive material eligible; establish a clearer narrative job and evidentiary label for each item, or move detail to the separate archive when it no longer advances the primary argument.

The third gap is engagement clarity. Email is findable, but the page offers little help answering “What sort of consequential problem should I bring?” A short, discreet invitation could orient the right visitor without turning the page into a consultancy sales pitch.

The intended mystique works best where it reflects legitimate discretion about an unnamed client or selective disclosure. It becomes mere ambiguity when project role, implementation stage, AI experience, or forum participation cannot be determined.

## Recommended next round

1. Ask the owner what publicly supportable detail can be added to PROGRESS: exact role, output, implementation stage, and whether any specific follow-on result can be named. Preserve source/date qualification.
2. Decide whether an applied AI example can be published. If not, present AI as an area of strategic method and thought, not as equally evidenced delivery.
3. Test a one-line engagement invitation and the position of the Media-archive link, without changing the no-image-limit rule or collapsing the archive into the homepage.
4. Test these copy/hierarchy revisions with **real prospective visitors**. GOV.UK's usual 4–8-person qualitative round supports issue discovery, not population percentages; recruit likely users with relevant variation. Include access needs where possible and pair observation with standards-based accessibility evaluation. See [GOV.UK's planning guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service), [moderated task guidance](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing), [NN/g on leading questions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/leading-questions/) and [W3C WAI on involving users](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/).

No production or review-page copy was changed during this pilot.
